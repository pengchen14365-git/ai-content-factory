# PRD Quality Checklist and Validation Standards

This comprehensive checklist helps ensure your Product Requirements Document meets quality standards across multiple dimensions. Use this checklist during the review process to validate completeness, clarity, and effectiveness of your PRD.

## Quality Framework Overview

The PRD quality framework evaluates five key dimensions:

1. **Completeness** (30% weight) - Comprehensive coverage of all necessary information
2. **Clarity** (25% weight) - Clear, readable, and unambiguous language
3. **Consistency** (20% weight) - Logical flow and consistent terminology
4. **Actionability** (15% weight) - Requirements that can be implemented and tested
5. **Structure** (10% weight) - Proper organization and formatting

## Quality Scoring System

### Scoring Scale
- **90-100**: Excellent - Outstanding quality, minimal improvements needed
- **80-89**: Good - High quality with minor issues
- **70-79**: Satisfactory - Meets basic requirements, needs improvements
- **60-69**: Needs Improvement - Significant issues that must be addressed
- **0-59**: Failing - Major problems requiring major rework

### Quality Gates
- **Critical Issues**: Any score below 60% in any dimension
- **Major Issues**: Any score below 70% in any dimension
- **Minor Issues**: Scores below 80% in any dimension
- **Excellent**: Overall score above 90%

## Detailed Quality Checklists

### 1. Completeness Checklist (30% weight)

#### Document Information
- [ ] Document has clear title and version information
- [ ] Creation and modification dates are current
- [ ] All team members and stakeholders are identified
- [ ] Document approval status is clear

#### Product Overview
- [ ] Product vision is clearly defined
- [ ] Business goals and objectives are specific and measurable
- [ ] Market context and problem statement are comprehensive
- [ ] Success metrics and key performance indicators (KPIs) are defined
- [ ] Competitive landscape analysis is included

#### User Analysis
- [ ] Target users are clearly defined and segmented
- [ ] User personas are detailed and realistic
- [ ] User needs and pain points are well-documented
- [ ] User journey maps are complete
- [ ] User research and validation data is included

#### Functional Requirements
- [ ] All features are clearly defined and prioritized
- [ ] User stories follow proper format and are testable
- [ ] Acceptance criteria are specific and measurable
- [ ] Edge cases and exceptions are considered
- [ ] Feature dependencies are identified
- [ ] Business rules are well-documented

#### Non-Functional Requirements
- [ ] Performance requirements are specific (response times, throughput)
- [ ] Security requirements are comprehensive
- [ ] Availability and reliability targets are defined
- [ ] Compatibility requirements are complete
- [ ] Scalability requirements are specified
- [ ] Usability and accessibility standards are defined

#### Technical Requirements
- [ ] Technology stack is appropriate and justified
- [ ] Architecture design is sound and scalable
- [ ] Data models and schemas are well-defined
- [ ] API specifications are complete
- [ ] Integration requirements are clear
- [ ] Deployment and infrastructure requirements are specified

#### Project Plan
- [ ] Timeline is realistic and detailed
- [ ] Resource allocation is appropriate
- [ ] Milestones and deliverables are clearly defined
- [ ] Risk assessment is comprehensive
- [ ] Budget considerations are addressed
- [ ] Dependencies are identified and planned

#### Quality Assurance
- [ ] Testing strategy is comprehensive
- [ ] Test cases are defined and testable
- [ ] Quality metrics are established
- [ ] Defect management process is defined
- [ ] User acceptance testing is planned
- [ ] Performance testing requirements are specified

### 2. Clarity Checklist (25% weight)

#### Language Quality
- [ ] Language is clear, concise, and straightforward
- [ ] Technical terms are defined or avoided
- [ ] Sentences are under 25 words (preferred)
- [ ] Active voice is used consistently
- [ ] No jargon or ambiguous terminology

#### Specificity and Detail
- [ ] Requirements are specific and not vague
- [ ] Examples are provided where helpful
- [ ] Measurements are quantitative, not qualitative
- [ ] User stories are detailed and complete
- [ ] Acceptance criteria are precise

#### Readability
- [ ] Document flows logically from section to section
- [ ] Headings and subheadings are clear and descriptive
- [ ] Lists and bullet points are used for readability
- [ ] Complex concepts are explained simply
- [ ] Document is appropriate length for the project scope

#### Visual Structure
- [ ] Document has clear visual hierarchy
- [ ] Consistent formatting is used throughout
- [ ] White space is used effectively
- [ ] Important information is emphasized appropriately
- [ ] Visual aids (diagrams, charts) are included where helpful

### 3. Consistency Checklist (20% weight)

#### Terminology Consistency
- [ ] Terms are used consistently throughout the document
- [ ] Definitions are clear and consistent
- [ ] No contradictory definitions exist
- [ ] Industry standards and conventions are followed
- [ ] Custom terms are clearly defined

#### Priority Consistency
- [ ] Priority levels are consistent and meaningful
- [ ] High/Medium/Low priority definitions are clear
- [ ] Priority assignments are justified
- [ ] No conflicting priority levels exist
- [ ] Priority scales are standardized

#### Requirement Consistency
- [ ] No contradictory requirements exist
- [ ] Dependencies are logical and consistent
- [ ] Assumptions are documented and consistent
- [ ] Requirements align with business goals
- [ ] Technical requirements support functional requirements

#### Formatting Consistency
- [ ] Formatting is consistent across sections
- [ ] Numbering and labeling is systematic
- [ ] Document structure follows logical patterns
- [ ] References and links are consistent
- [ ] Date formats are standardized

### 4. Actionability Checklist (15% weight)

#### Implementability
- [ ] Requirements can be technically implemented
- [ ] Implementation approach is feasible
- [ ] Resources required are realistic
- [ ] Timeframes are achievable
- [ ] Dependencies are manageable

#### Testability
- [ ] All requirements can be tested
- [ ] Acceptance criteria are measurable
- [ ] Test cases can be derived from requirements
- [ ] Success criteria are clear and specific
- [ ] Testing approach is practical

#### Ownership
- [ ] Clear ownership is defined for requirements
- [ ] Responsibilities are assigned
- [ ] Decision-makers are identified
- [ ] Stakeholders are clearly defined
- [ ] Escalation paths are established

#### Priority and Planning
- [ ] Requirements are prioritized logically
- [ ] Implementation sequence is planned
- [ ] Dependencies are considered in planning
- [ ] Risk mitigation is planned
- [ ] Success criteria are defined

### 5. Structure Checklist (10% weight)

#### Organization
- [ ] Document follows logical structure
- [ ] Sections flow naturally from one to another
- [ ] Content is organized hierarchically
- [ ] Related information is grouped together
- [ ] Cross-references are used appropriately

#### Completeness of Structure
- [ ] All required sections are present
- [ ] Section hierarchy is appropriate
- [ ] No missing sections or gaps
- [ ] Section titles are descriptive
- [ ] Document begins and ends appropriately

#### Navigation
- [ ] Table of contents is included
- [ ] Page numbers are consistent
- [ ] Hyperlinks and references work
- [ ] Document is easy to navigate
- [ ] Important information is easy to find

#### Professional Appearance
- [ ] Document looks professional and polished
- [ ] Consistent formatting throughout
- [ ] No spelling or grammar errors
- [ ] Visual elements are appropriate
- [ ] Document is ready for stakeholder review

## Quality Review Process

### Pre-Review Preparation
1. **Self-Assessment**
   - Complete the checklist independently
   - Score each dimension honestly
   - Identify major concerns and issues

2. **Team Review**
   - Have team members complete the checklist
   - Compare scores and identify discrepancies
   - Discuss areas of concern

3. **Stakeholder Review**
   - Get input from key stakeholders
   - Ensure business alignment
   - Validate user requirements

### Review Meeting Process
1. **Opening Discussion**
   - Review overall quality scores
   - Discuss major issues and concerns
   - Establish review priorities

2. **Section-by-Section Review**
   - Work through each section systematically
   - Apply the checklist to each section
   - Document issues and recommendations

3. **Action Planning**
   - Prioritize issues by severity
   - Assign responsibilities for fixes
   - Set deadlines for corrections
   - Schedule follow-up review

4. **Final Validation**
   - Verify all critical issues are resolved
   - Confirm quality gates are met
   - Get final approval and sign-off

## Common Quality Issues and Solutions

### Critical Issues (Must Fix)
**Problem**: Missing core sections or critical requirements
**Solution**: Add missing sections with comprehensive content
**Impact**: High - Can derail entire project

**Problem**: Contradictory requirements
**Solution**: Resolve conflicts through stakeholder discussions
**Impact**: High - Can cause development delays

**Problem**: Unimplementable requirements
**Solution**: Validate feasibility with technical team
**Impact**: High - Can make project impossible

### Major Issues (Should Fix)
**Problem**: Vague or ambiguous language
**Solution**: Replace with specific, measurable terms
**Impact**: Medium - Can lead to misunderstandings

**Problem**: Missing user research
**Solution**: Conduct user research or document assumptions
**Impact**: Medium - Can result in wrong product direction

**Problem**: Incomplete acceptance criteria
**Solution**: Add specific, testable criteria
**Impact**: Medium - Can lead to incomplete features

### Minor Issues (Nice to Fix)
**Problem**: Formatting inconsistencies
**Solution**: Standardize formatting throughout
**Impact**: Low - Affects readability but not content

**Problem**: Minor grammatical errors
**Solution**: Proofread and edit
**Impact**: Low - Doesn't affect meaning

**Problem**: Missing visual aids
**Solution**: Add diagrams and charts for clarity
**Impact**: Low - Enhances understanding

## Quality Improvement Strategies

### Preventive Measures
1. **Template Usage**
   - Use standardized PRD templates
   - Include quality checklist in templates
   - Build quality requirements into process

2. **Early Validation**
   - Validate requirements with stakeholders early
   - Conduct technical feasibility assessments
   - Test user understanding of requirements

3. **Regular Reviews**
   - Schedule regular quality checkpoints
   - Conduct peer reviews
   - Use automated quality checks

### Continuous Improvement
1. **Feedback Collection**
   - Collect feedback from reviewers
   - Track common issues and patterns
   - Use feedback to improve templates

2. **Training**
   - Provide PRD writing training
   - Share best practices
   - Create knowledge base of examples

3. **Metrics Tracking**
   - Track quality scores over time
   - Monitor time spent on rework
   - Measure requirement stability
